# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent. Assume good faith. Keep disagreements about the work rather than the person.

## What is expected

- Take other people's time seriously. A clear issue, a reproducible bug report and a focused
  pull request are all forms of respect.
- Accept that people have different levels of experience, different first languages, and
  different amounts of time. This project ships in three languages; not everyone reading it
  is comfortable in English.
- Disagree about the substance. "This breaks right-to-left layout because…" is useful;
  "this is obviously wrong" is not.
- Assume the other person had a reason. Ask what it was.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, personal attacks, or demeaning comments — public or private.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, reopening settled decisions without new
  information, or demanding unpaid work from maintainers.
- Anything that would reasonably make someone feel unwelcome for who they are rather than
  what they wrote.

## Scope

Anywhere this project happens: issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages, and any
space where you are representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately by opening a
[security advisory](https://github.com/nazilafeef/dheys-cms/security/advisories/new) — it is
the private channel this repository has, and it works for conduct reports too. Say what
happened and link to it if it is public.

Reports are handled confidentially. You will get an acknowledgement, and you will be told
what was decided.

## Enforcement

In proportion, and usually in this order:

1. A private word, explaining what was wrong and why.
2. A public correction, where the behaviour was public.
3. A temporary ban from participation.
4. A permanent ban.

Serious behaviour — harassment, threats, publishing private information — skips straight to
the end. Maintainers who break this document are held to it more firmly, not less.
